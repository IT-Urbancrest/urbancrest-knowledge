# Disconnected

**Speaker:** Geoff Prows  
**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Primary Scripture:** Jeremiah 7:1-11  
**Series:** Bad Religion

## Message Summary

In **Disconnected**, the second message of **Bad Religion**, Geoff Prows taught from Jeremiah 7:1-11. Judah's people came to the temple and continued their religious routines, but their worship had become disconnected from the way they lived. They trusted the presence of the temple and their religious activity while continuing to mistreat others and disobey God.

Geoff compared this kind of religion to a security system that lights up and makes noise but is disconnected from monitoring. Everything looks as though it is working, yet the connection is missing. The same danger exists when church attendance, familiar practices, and outward worship take the place of a real relationship with God.

The message identified three ways disconnection shows up: **misplaced trust, misguided behavior, and misunderstood repentance**. God calls His people to trust Him, let their faith shape their relationships, and turn from sin. Religious activity cannot substitute for genuine faith and obedience.

Geoff emphasized that obedience does not earn salvation. Changed behavior flows from an authentic relationship with God. Grace welcomes broken people and leads them toward a new direction; it does not give permission to remain comfortable in sin. Growth takes time, but faith in Christ produces a desire to live for Him.

The invitation was to reconnect worship with daily life, faith with obedience, and our lives with Jesus.

## Main Idea

**Authentic faith changes how you live because authentic faith cannot be disconnected from daily living.**

## Message Outline

### I. Through Misplaced Trust

Jeremiah 7:4 and 7:8 expose the people's false confidence. They treated the temple as a guarantee of safety and their religious performance as a way to secure God's favor. Their trust was in a place and a practice rather than in God Himself.

Geoff connected this warning to church attendance, membership, and baptism. These cannot replace personal faith in Jesus. Baptism pictures the work Christ has done in a believer's life; it does not save a person or wash away sin.

Misplaced trust also appears in daily life when believers cling to control over finances, schedules, plans, or outcomes instead of surrendering them to God. Psalm 20:7 points us away from confidence in human strength and toward trust in the Lord.

### II. Through Misguided Behavior

In Jeremiah 7:3-9, God tells the people to amend their ways and deeds. Their lives were marked by injustice, oppression, dishonesty, theft, adultery, violence, and idolatry. What they professed in worship did not match the way they treated others.

Geoff warned against assuming that Sunday worship compensates for Monday-through-Saturday disobedience. We all need God's grace, and broken people should come to God and the church. The problem in Jeremiah was that the people were comfortable in sin rather than seeking forgiveness and change.

Love, integrity, fairness, and forgiveness flow from a relationship with God. They do not earn His favor or replace faith in Christ. Worship is meant to shape how we treat people throughout the week.

### III. Through Misunderstood Repentance

Repentance means turning from sin and self toward the Lord. It includes sorrow over sin, but it goes beyond feeling bad. Geoff described a change of mind that leads to a change of heart and action.

The people assumed that worship gave them immunity to keep living however they wanted. Jeremiah 7:10-11 challenges that thinking. God saves people from sin, not so they can continue treating sin as acceptable.

Geoff used 2 Chronicles 7:14, Acts 3:19, and 2 Corinthians 7:10 to show that repentance involves returning to God and changing direction. He also pointed to John 8:11: forgiveness and the call to leave sin behind belong together. Grace should move us toward holy living.

## Grace and Growth

Authentic faith does not mean a believer immediately becomes perfect. Geoff acknowledged that sanctification, the process of becoming more like Jesus, takes a lifetime. The question is whether Christ has brought change and whether our hearts desire to live His way, even when we do not get everything right.

Our confidence belongs in Jesus. The fruit of that relationship is a life increasingly shaped by trust, repentance, love, and obedience.

## Questions for Personal Reflection

- How has your faith in Christ changed how you live?
- Does your Monday-through-Saturday life match your Sunday worship?
- Where have you placed your daily trust? Where have you placed your ultimate, eternal trust?
- Have you become disconnected from God, and what would it look like to reconnect today?

# The Lord's Prayer

**Speaker:** Tucker Howard  
**Date:** 2026-08-16  
**Primary Scripture:** Matthew 6:9-15 (CSB)  
**Series:** Summer on the Mount: Kingdom Living for the Here and Now

## Message Summary

Pastor Tucker Howard taught that the Lord's Prayer is not merely a set of words to recite. Jesus gave it as a model that teaches His followers how to pray and whom to depend on. Located at the center of the Sermon on the Mount, the prayer points believers toward a life centered on prayer and complete dependence on God.

The prayer begins with a personal relationship with God as Father, honors His holiness, and asks for His kingdom and will rather than our own. It teaches daily dependence on God's provision, regular confession of sin, forgiveness toward others, and reliance on God for protection from temptation and the evil one.

## Main Idea

The Lord's Prayer is a model of complete dependence on God that reshapes how believers approach the Father, seek His kingdom, trust His provision, forgive others, and face temptation.

## Official Fill-In Outline

### “Our Father”

Jesus teaches His disciples to approach God personally as Father. Prayer is not a performance but an expression of dependence, trusting that the Father knows, cares, and invites His children to know Him.

### “Your Name be Honored as holy”

God's name is honored when His people depend on Him rather than merely performing religious acts. How believers understand God shapes the way they pray.

### “Your Kingdom Come”

This request includes submitting to God's rule, praying for the gospel to advance and disciples to be made, and looking expectantly toward Christ's return. Praying for the coming kingdom reminds believers that God's work is **not finished** and that the best is yet to come.

### “Your will be done on earth as it is in heaven”

Believers surrender their own agendas to the lordship of Jesus and ask God to use ordinary people, ordinary work, and ordinary days to accomplish His restoring purposes. The posture of faithful prayer is, “Not my will, but Yours be done.”

### “Give us today our daily bread”

Daily bread includes physical provision and serves as an invitation to daily dependence. Instead of trusting paychecks, plans, possessions, or personal ability, believers acknowledge God as the giver of everything they need and Jesus as the Bread of Life.

### “And forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven our debtors”

Sin creates a debt people cannot repay, but Jesus paid that debt through the cross. Believers confess sin regularly, not because salvation is repeatedly lost, but because healthy fellowship with the Father includes ongoing confession. **Forgiven people forgive people.**

### “And do not bring us into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one”

God does not tempt anyone. This request recognizes that believers cannot overcome sinful desires and spiritual opposition through self-confidence. They need God's protection and deliverance, depending on a Savior rather than merely asking for assistance.

### “For if you forgive others their offenses...”

Jesus returns to forgiveness because unforgiveness reveals the heart. Forgiving others does not earn God's forgiveness; instead, a heart transformed by the gospel becomes a forgiving heart. **Grace receivers become grace givers.**

## Closing Applications from the Fill-In Notes

### 1. Examine How You Pray

Howard challenged believers to consider what would change if God answered every prayer they prayed in the previous twenty-four hours. A weak prayer life may reveal weak dependence on God.

### 2. Surrender Your Will

Followers of Jesus must choose between the posture of Eden, “Not as You will, but as I will,” and the posture of Gethsemane, “Not as I will, but as You will.”

### 3. Forgive Your Debtors

Believers are called to forgive because they have been forgiven. Howard urged listeners not to allow unforgiveness and bitterness to become a prison they choose to live in.

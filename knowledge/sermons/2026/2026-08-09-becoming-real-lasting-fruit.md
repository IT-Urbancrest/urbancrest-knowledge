# Becoming Real, Lasting Fruit

Speaker: Jerry Shannon  
Date: 2026-08-09  
Primary Scripture: Matthew 6:1-8 (ESV)  
Series: Summer on the Mount: Kingdom Living for the Here and Now

## Message Summary

Guest speaker Jerry Shannon taught from Matthew 6:1-8 that Jesus is concerned with more than outwardly impressive religious behavior. Kingdom righteousness must be genuine from the heart. Using the picture of a real apple beside a decorative imitation, Shannon challenged believers to become "real lasting fruit" rather than simply looking faithful on the outside.

The message emphasized humility, eternal priorities, generosity, care for people in need, and private devotion to God. Shannon repeatedly returned to the idea that God's generosity toward us should transform the way we hold our resources and respond to others.

## Main Idea

Jesus calls His followers to authentic kingdom living that flows from a transformed heart. Instead of living for recognition, comfort, or control, believers are called to humility, open-handed generosity, eternal rewards, and an intimate relationship with God as their true audience.

## Sermon Outline

### 1. Allow My Pride to Be Exposed

Jesus warns against practicing righteousness in order to be seen by others. Pride can turn good actions into performances centered on recognition, control, comfort, or personal benefit.

Shannon pointed believers toward the humility described in Philippians 2:3-5, valuing others above ourselves and adopting the mindset of Christ.

### 2. Choose Delayed Rewards

Jesus contrasts receiving recognition from people now with receiving reward from the Father.

The message challenged believers to consider which reward they are actually seeking. Kingdom living willingly gives up present applause and personal advantage in order to pursue what matters eternally.

### 3. Be Transformed by God's Generosity

In Matthew 6, Jesus says "when you give to the needy," treating generosity as an expected part of faithful discipleship.

Shannon emphasized that everything ultimately belongs to God and that every person is made in God's image. God's generosity toward us should reshape our hearts toward people in need.

Ephesians 4:28 was used to show the transformation of a person from taking for self to working so that there is something to share with others.

### 4. Make Generosity My Default Setting

Generosity should become an increasingly natural expression of a life transformed by Christ.

Shannon connected Matthew 6 with James 2 and Deuteronomy 15, challenging believers to move away from hard-heartedness and tight-fistedness toward open-handed care for people in need.

He also pointed to the early church in Acts, where believers shared possessions and resources as an outward expression of genuine faith.

### 5. Draw Close to an Audience of One

Jesus' teaching about giving and prayer in secret points believers toward a deep relationship with the Father.

Rather than being controlled by other people's opinions, praise, or expectations, followers of Jesus are called to live before God as their primary audience. Authentic faith asks God to examine the motives behind religious activity and transform the heart.

## Key Biblical Assumptions Emphasized in the Message

- God owns everything, including what we consider ours.
- Every person is made in God's image.
- Followers of Jesus are called to recognize God's image in others.
- Christians are called to imitate God's character.
- God sees both outward actions and inward motives.
- God, rather than wealth or possessions, is the believer's ultimate security.

## Generosity and People in Need

A major theme of the message was God's concern for people experiencing poverty and need.

Shannon referenced Proverbs 19:17 and Proverbs 28:27, James 2, Deuteronomy 15, and the generosity of the early church to argue that care for people in need is not peripheral to Christian discipleship. He challenged believers to hold possessions with open hands and allow God to direct how their resources are used.

The sermon closed with an invitation to surrender every area of security and possession to Jesus, asking Him to make generosity a natural expression of His work in us.

## Guest Speaker Context

Jerry Shannon serves as Director of **A Child's Hope International**, an organization Urbancrest supports. Before the sermon, he shared about the ministry's work providing food, clean water, and holistic care locally and globally, including work in Cincinnati, Malawi, the Dominican Republic, and Guatemala.

# Logs, Dogs, and Hogs

**Speaker:** Geoff Prows  
**Date:** 2026-09-06  
**Primary Scripture:** Matthew 7:1-6, 15-16 (NASB 1995)  
**Series:** Summer on the Mount: Kingdom Living for the Here and Now

## Message Summary

Pastor Geoff Prows taught that Jesus' command, "Do not judge," does not require believers to abandon biblical standards, critical thinking, or discernment. Jesus Himself warns His followers to recognize false prophets by their fruit. What Jesus forbids is a harsh, condemning, unmerciful, and hypocritical spirit that assumes God's place as judge.

Kingdom citizens should reject judgmentalism because it is foolish, prideful, and hypocritical. People rarely know every fact, often carry biases, and will themselves answer to God. Pride makes a person scrutinize a small fault in someone else while remaining blind to serious sin in his or her own life. Correction without honest self-examination cannot heal and instead causes harm.

Jesus calls His followers to examine themselves first and then help others with gentleness, mercy, and restoration as the goal. He also calls them to holy discernment. Matthew 7:6 is not a command to withhold the gospel selfishly, but a warning to recognize when a person has become hardened and hostile. In such a moment, continuing to argue may cause more harm than good.

## Main Idea

Jesus calls believers to abandon foolish, prideful, and hypocritical judgmentalism while practicing self-examination, gentle correction, and holy discernment.

## Official Fill-In Outline

### I. Foolish

Harsh judgment is foolish because the standard a person applies to others invites judgment in return. Only Jesus is qualified to redeem or condemn, and every believer will stand before God's judgment seat. People also lack complete knowledge and may reach conclusions based on partial facts, prejudice, confirmation bias, favoritism, or a single negative impression.

Prows emphasized that faultfinders are not reliable fact-finders. Believers should remember that there is often more to another person's story than they can see and should extend the patience they themselves need from others.

### II. Prideful

Jesus' picture of a speck and a log exposes pride. A speck is small enough that someone must search for it, yet pride makes a person hunt for another's fault while overlooking a much larger problem in his or her own life. What people choose to look for often determines what they find.

Pride blinds people to their own sin and tempts them to become "speck inspectors." Prows challenged listeners to look honestly at their motives and their own need for repentance before focusing on someone else's failures.

### III. Hypocritical

A hypocrite is like an actor wearing a mask. Jesus says a person must first remove the log from his or her own eye and only then help with the speck in another's eye. This is not a rejection of correction, but the proper order and spirit for it.

Biblical correction begins with self-examination and proceeds with gentleness. Its purpose is restoration, not condemnation. Believers should be severe with themselves and gracious with others, quick to repent and slow to rebuke.

## Judgmentalism and Discernment

Matthew 7:6 brings balance to Jesus' warning against judgmentalism. The holy thing and valuable pearls point to the gospel. Jesus is not teaching believers to be stingy with the good news, but to recognize when someone is not merely unreceptive but hardened and hostile.

Prows pointed to Jesus' instruction to shake the dust from one's feet and Paul's decision to turn to the Gentiles after persistent resistance and blasphemy. Faithful witness requires a tender heart and a discerning mind: boldness to speak God's truth, wisdom to recognize the right moment, and gentleness to wait when a person is not ready.

## A Practical Test Before Speaking

Prows offered the acrostic NEED as a way to examine potentially critical words:

- Is it **Necessary**?
- Will it **Encourage**?
- Will it **Edify**?
- Will it **Dignify** the other person?

If the answer is no, it does not need to be said.

## The Gospel and Christ's Righteousness

True faith abandons self-righteousness because every person has sinned and fallen short of God's glory. People are not made right with God by their own goodness or works, but through faith in Jesus Christ. Jesus took sinners' guilt so that those who trust Him can be clothed in His righteousness, forgiven, and restored to God.

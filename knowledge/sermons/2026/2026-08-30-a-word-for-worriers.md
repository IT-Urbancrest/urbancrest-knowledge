# A Word for Worriers

**Speaker:** Geoff Prows  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Primary Scripture:** Matthew 6:25-34 (NASB 1995)  
**Series:** Summer on the Mount: Kingdom Living for the Here and Now

## Message Summary

Pastor Geoff Prows taught that worry divides the heart, draws people in competing directions, and pulls them away from trusting God. Jesus commands His followers not to worry, then gives them concrete reasons they can entrust their needs, their future, and their lives to their heavenly Father.

Using Jesus' pictures of the birds of the air and the lilies of the field, Prows emphasized that God cares for His creation and cares even more for people made in His image. Worry cannot add anything useful to life, but God already knows every need and promises to provide as His people seek His kingdom and righteousness first.

## Main Idea

Believers do not have to worry because their heavenly Father knows their needs, cares for them, and promises to provide as they make Christ and His kingdom their first priority.

## Official Fill-In Outline

### We Do Not Have to Worry!

Jesus repeats the command not to worry because worry draws people away from God and divides their attention between trust and fear. Prows described worry as a faith issue rooted in doubt that God can handle the problems His people face.

### I. God Is Our Heavenly Father

Food, drink, and clothing are legitimate needs, but they are not the purpose of life. The birds and lilies demonstrate the Father's care for His creation. If earthly parents seek to provide for their children, the heavenly Father can be trusted to care perfectly for His children and give what is good.

### II. Worry Is Worthless

Worry cannot extend a person's life or solve a problem. It may consume time, damage health, disturb peace, and affect others, but it accomplishes nothing useful. Believers can act on matters within their responsibility and entrust what they cannot control to God.

### III. God Knows What I Need

God knows every need before it surprises or even becomes apparent to His people. He is not indifferent, distracted, or caught off guard. Because the sovereign God sees, knows, and cares, His children do not need to live like those who have no heavenly Father.

### IV. God's Promise to Provide

Jesus commands His followers to seek God's kingdom and righteousness continually and promises that their needs will be supplied. The necessities of life are not to become the priority of life. Believers are called to give Jesus their today and leave tomorrow with God.

## Seeking First the Kingdom

Prows challenged listeners to identify what competes with Jesus for first place in their lives. Work, success, possessions, and other responsibilities may matter, but a relationship with Christ must remain the central priority.

God's ultimate provision is Jesus Christ. Beyond daily needs, every person needs forgiveness and restoration to God. Through Christ, believers receive salvation, peace, joy, and confidence that their Father will care for them.

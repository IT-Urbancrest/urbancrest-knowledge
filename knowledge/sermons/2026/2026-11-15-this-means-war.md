# This Means War

**Guest Speaker:** Thomas Howard  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Primary Scripture:** 2 Corinthians 10:3–5 (CSB)  
**Series:** Standalone message

## Message Summary

Our thoughts can shape how we see ourselves, how we respond to others, and what we believe about God. Following the student ministry's Fall Retreat weekend, themed **Captive**, guest speaker Thomas Howard challenged us to recognize the spiritual battle for our minds and depend on the strength God provides. Drawing from 2 Corinthians 10 and Ephesians 6, he reminded us that freedom comes through Christ and His Word, rather than simply trying harder on our own.

## Main Idea

Victory in the battlefield of our mind is not found in human wisdom, intellect, or discipline, but in the weapons found in Christ and His Word.

## Message Outline

### 1. Our battle is not of this world.

Paul explains that although we live in the flesh, we do not wage war according to the flesh. Ephesians 6:12 reminds us that our struggle reaches beyond the people and circumstances around us. Thomas encouraged listeners to recognize the enemy's lies and resist the temptation to face spiritual battles through willpower alone.

### 2. God has provided Christians supernaturally powerful weapons.

God has given believers what they need to stand firm. Thomas emphasized that we fight from the victory Christ has already secured, and he walked through the full armor of God in Ephesians 6:10–17:

- **The belt of truth:** Walk closely with Jesus, who is the truth. Religious activity cannot replace a daily relationship with Him.
- **The breastplate of righteousness:** Rest in the righteousness Christ gives. Our identity is rooted in Him, rather than our past mistakes, accomplishments, appearance, or status.
- **Readiness for the gospel of peace:** Be ready to share the good news. Thomas challenged parents to make disciples in their homes and believers to carry the gospel into everyday relationships.
- **The shield of faith:** Trust God's character and promises when temptation, doubt, and discouragement come. Jesus satisfies in ways the world cannot.
- **The helmet of salvation:** Remind yourself of the gospel. God's grace and love give us a foundation when doubts challenge what He has said.
- **The sword of the Spirit:** Know, love, and use God's Word. Scripture equips us to recognize lies and respond with truth.

Thomas urged listeners to put on the whole armor, including the parts that challenge their comfort or require action.

### 3. We must take our thoughts captive or they will take us captive.

Taking a thought captive means bringing it before Christ and examining it in light of Scripture. Thomas invited listeners to identify thoughts marked by shame, bitterness, unforgiveness, temptation, or discouragement and ask whether those thoughts agree with God's truth.

He shared how Scripture had challenged his own discouragement. Galatians 6:9 reminded him to keep doing good without giving up. First John 4:4 reminded him that the One living in believers is greater than the one in the world. These examples showed how God's Word can speak directly to the lies we begin to believe.

## A Call to Respond

Thomas invited each person to identify a thought that may be holding them captive, write it down, and bring it under the truth of God's Word. He encouraged listeners to turn down the world's noise, make room to hear from the Lord, and talk with a staff member, Sunday School class, or Small Group when they need support. His closing encouragement was that God's grace is greater than our sin, and He desires His children to live in the freedom found in the gospel.

# Trivial Pursuits: Chasing What Doesn’t Last

**Speaker:** Geoff Prows  
**Date:** 2026-08-23  
**Primary Scripture:** Matthew 6:19-24 (NASB 1995)  
**Series:** Summer on the Mount: Kingdom Living for the Here and Now

## Message Summary

Pastor Geoff Prows taught that followers of Jesus must be distinguished from the world by what they pursue. Using the board game Trivial Pursuit as an illustration, he warned that many people spend their lives chasing personal happiness, wealth, position, recognition, comfort, or retirement while neglecting what will matter eternally.

In Matthew 6:19-24, Jesus uses three contrasts to expose the heart: two treasures, two eyes, and two masters. Earthly treasure is temporary and vulnerable, while heavenly treasure lasts. Clear spiritual vision produces single devotion and generosity, while a divided and stingy heart darkens a person's view of life. Ultimately, every person serves a master, and no one can serve both God and wealth.

## Main Idea

Kingdom citizens must stop chasing what will not last and pursue eternal treasure through generous stewardship and undivided devotion to Jesus as their only Master.

## Official Fill-In Outline

### I. Two Treasures

Jesus contrasts earthly treasure, which can decay, disappear, or be stolen, with heavenly treasure that cannot be taken away. The command is not that possessing money or material things is inherently sinful, but that believers must not allow possessions to possess them or place their hope in uncertain riches.

What people treasure reveals the condition of their hearts. Prows challenged listeners to ask what fills their daydreams, what they fear losing most, and what they believe they cannot be happy without. Those answers expose whether they are exchanging eternal priorities for temporary pursuits.

### II. Two Eyes

The eye represents the spiritual window through which a person sees life. The clear eye is both generous and singly devoted to God, while the bad eye is stingy and divided.

Believers recognize that everything belongs to God and that they are stewards rather than owners. This frees them from hoarding and makes generosity a natural expression of devotion. An ungenerous spirit clouds a person's view of happiness, success, and God's will.

### III. Two Masters

Jesus states that no one can serve two masters. Every person is already serving someone or something, but only one master can hold first place.

Prows explained that Jesus does not merely want a prominent place in a believer's life but the preeminent place. Wealth, possessions, self, and worldly desires cannot share mastery with Christ. Paradoxically, complete surrender to Jesus brings true freedom because the Son sets His people free from slavery to sin.

## Pursuing What Lasts

The message called believers to store up heavenly treasure through faithful prayer, love for enemies, endurance in persecution, humble fasting, generosity, good works, and obedience to Christ. Money and possessions are gifts to steward, but they are uncertain and cannot provide lasting satisfaction or security.

Prows closed by asking who or what is the true master of each person's life. He invited listeners to stop pursuing what the world promises, recognize Jesus as Lord, and become rich toward God.

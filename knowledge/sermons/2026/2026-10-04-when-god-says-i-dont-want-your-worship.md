# When God Says, “I Don’t Want Your Worship”

**Speaker:** Geoff Prows  
**Date:** 2026-10-04  
**Primary Scripture:** Amos 5:21-27  
**Series:** Bad Religion

## Message Summary

In the opening message of **Bad Religion**, Geoff Prows taught from Amos 5:21-27, where God tells Israel that He hates and rejects their festivals, offerings, and songs. The people were highly religious. They gathered, gave, observed their festivals, and filled their worship with music, but their outward worship did not match the condition of their hearts or the way they treated other people.

Geoff emphasized that the problem was not the feasts, assemblies, offerings, or music themselves. The problem was the people. Their worship had become routine, self-focused, and disconnected from righteous living. They could honor God outwardly while their hearts remained far from Him. The danger is just as real for longtime believers today: familiar church rhythms can go on autopilot while the heart grows comfortable and distant from God.

Amos 5:24 shows what God delights in instead: **justice** and **righteousness** flowing through everyday life. Israel's failure to care for the poor and helpless, along with immorality and injustice, exposed the disconnect between their worship and their lives. Worship cannot be separated from the way people treat their families, coworkers, fellow church members, and others around them.

The final verses show God's discipline. Israel had not only distorted its worship of the Lord but had also embraced idolatry. God announced exile as discipline. Geoff described the purpose of God's discipline as restoration and purification rather than indifference toward sin.

The central takeaway was clear: **God wants more than our worship. He wants our heart. He wants our lives.** True worship is not something Christians do only on Sunday. It is lived Monday through Saturday through obedience, love, justice, righteousness, and integrity.

The message concluded by asking listeners whether they have a real relationship with God or simply religion. Religious activity cannot reconcile anyone to God. Jesus Christ does. Church attendance, giving, singing, and rule-keeping cannot bring a person to the Father. Salvation comes through Jesus, the mediator who brings sinners into right relationship with God.

## Main Idea

God wants more than our worship. He wants our heart and our lives.

## Message Outline

### I. God's Dislike (Amos 5:21-23)

Israel had no shortage of religious activity. The people observed festivals, gathered for solemn assemblies, brought offerings, and made music. Yet God said He hated and rejected their worship.

Geoff explained that their worship had become about themselves and had settled into routine. The people were going through the motions while their hearts were distant from God. Outward worship can look right while inwardly a person's life contradicts what that worship claims.

The warning applies especially to people who have been around church for a long time. Familiar patterns can become automatic. Knowing the songs, the language, and the order of a service is not the same thing as giving God the heart.

### II. God's Delight (Amos 5:24)

God says, **“Let justice roll down like waters and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream.”**

Geoff explained justice as doing what is right toward others, especially those who are vulnerable or easily mistreated. Righteousness is a life marked by mercy, generosity, honesty, goodness, and integrity that reflects God's character.

Israel wanted to compartmentalize worship from everyday relationships. Amos shows that God does not accept that separation. A person cannot claim to love God while living without love toward other people. Worship is meant to shape the way believers treat family members, coworkers, fellow church members, and everyone around them.

God is not impressed by worship that is separated from righteous and obedient living. He delights when worship and life belong together.

### III. God's Discipline (Amos 5:25-27)

Israel's history showed that the problem went deeper than empty ritual. The people had also turned toward idols and false gods. Because their worship and their hearts were far from God, judgment was coming and Israel would be sent into exile.

Geoff described God's discipline as having restoration as its goal. God does not ignore the spiritual condition of His people. He confronts sin rather than allowing empty religion and idolatry to continue unchecked.

## A Call to Examine Our Worship

Geoff gave several questions for personal reflection:

- Have my religious habits simply become routine?
- Am I going through the motions?
- Does the way I live Monday through Saturday reflect what I sing about on Sunday?
- Does my faith affect the way I treat my spouse, children, coworkers, and fellow church members?
- Have I compartmentalized worship and the rest of life into separate boxes?
- Do I have a relationship with God, or do I simply have religion?

## Religion and Relationship

The message ended by pointing listeners to Jesus Christ. Sin separates people from God, and religious activity cannot repair that separation.

Jesus did not come merely to give people another religious system to follow. He came so that sinners could have a relationship with God. Jesus is the way to the Father. Gathering, giving, singing, knowing church language, or keeping rules cannot earn salvation.

Religion focuses on practices and performance. Authentic faith calls on Jesus. God's grace reaches people where they are, and everyone who calls on the name of the Lord can be saved.

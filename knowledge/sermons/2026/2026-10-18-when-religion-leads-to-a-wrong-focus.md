# When Religion Leads to a Wrong Focus

**Speaker:** Geoff Prows  
**Date:** October 18, 2026  
**Primary Scripture:** Luke 11:37-44  
**Series:** Bad Religion, week three

## Message Summary

In **When Religion Leads to a Wrong Focus**, Geoff Prows taught from Luke 11:37-44 about the difference between impressive religious activity and authentic faith. A Pharisee invited Jesus to lunch and was shocked when Jesus did not perform the customary ceremonial washing before the meal. Jesus used that moment to expose a deeper problem: the Pharisees carefully maintained their outward appearance while neglecting the condition of their hearts.

Geoff opened with Mary and Martha, contrasting being busy doing things for Jesus with making time to be with Him. Religious activity is not the goal in itself. God wants a life that reflects His character and His heart.

The message identified three ways bad religion misdirects our focus: **the external instead of the internal, the less important while neglecting the more important, and personal glory instead of God's glory**. Jesus calls us beyond looking spiritual to receiving the inward transformation that changes how we love, give, serve, and treat people.

Geoff closed by asking listeners to examine themselves rather than simply criticize the Pharisees. Our neighbors, coworkers, and people who have been hurt by religion are watching whether the Jesus we proclaim is reflected in the lives we live. The question is not whether we are perfect, but whether our lives are moving in His direction.

## Main Idea

**Authentic faith grows from a heart changed by Jesus, values what God values, and seeks His glory rather than our own.**

## Message Outline

### I. Bad Religion Will Lead You to Focus on the External Instead of the Internal

In Luke 11:38-41, the Pharisee focused on Jesus' failure to observe a ceremonial handwashing tradition. Geoff explained that this was a question of ritual purity, not hygiene. The Pharisees expected others to follow a man-made tradition, but Jesus addressed the greed and wickedness within them.

Jesus compared their religion to a cup or plate that looks clean on the outside while remaining dirty within. People can learn church language, become familiar with religious practices, and look the part without allowing God to transform their hearts. God sees the attitudes and motives that others cannot see, including bitterness behind a smile and pride behind service.

Geoff explained that Jesus' words about giving to the poor do not teach that good works purchase cleansing or salvation. Mercy, compassion, and generosity should flow from a heart that has been made right with God. The call is to pursue the God of holiness rather than merely the appearance of holiness.

### II. Bad Religion Will Lead You to Focus on the Less Important While Neglecting the More Important

In Luke 11:42, Jesus confronted the Pharisees for carefully tithing even their garden herbs while disregarding justice and the love of God. Jesus did not tell them to abandon generosity. He told them not to neglect the greater responsibilities of loving God and treating people rightly.

Geoff connected this warning to church life. Programs, methods, traditions, and personal preferences can become more important to us than the mission they are meant to serve. A church can become absorbed in how it does things while losing sight of reaching people and becoming the people God calls it to be.

Doctrine, biblical truth, worship, and gathering together still matter. The warning is against letting lesser concerns distract us from justice, kindness, humility, and love. Micah 6:8 points to the kind of life God desires. Geoff summarized the danger as counting leaves while neglecting lives.

### III. Bad Religion Will Lead You to Focus on Personal Glory Instead of God's Glory

In Luke 11:43, Jesus exposed the Pharisees' love of prominent seats and respectful public greetings. Their religious status had become a way to gain recognition and elevate themselves.

Geoff described a daily struggle over whose glory we seek. We may say that our service is all about Jesus while still craving credit, admiration, or acknowledgment. A helpful test is whether we would keep serving if no one noticed.

Authentic faith leads to humble service that makes much of Jesus. He came as a servant, and His followers are called to follow His example. In 1 Thessalonians 2:5-6, Paul describes ministry that does not seek praise from people. Serving Christ is an opportunity to honor Him, not a way to demand honor for ourselves.

## The Influence of Our Faith

In Luke 11:44, Jesus compared the Pharisees to concealed tombs. Geoff explained the background from Numbers 19: contact with a grave made a person ceremonially unclean. An unmarked grave could affect someone who did not realize it was there.

The image warns that bad religion harms others as well as ourselves. Outward religious performance can teach people that Christianity is about keeping up appearances rather than a relationship with Jesus that transforms the heart. Our lives should help people see His truth, grace, humility, and love.

## Questions for Personal Reflection

- Am I more concerned with looking holy than with allowing God to change my heart?
- What attitudes or motives have I hidden behind religious activity?
- Have my preferences or traditions become more important to me than loving people and sharing Jesus?
- Would I keep serving if no one noticed or gave me credit?
- What are others learning about Jesus from the way I live?
- Is there a Pharisee in me, and how will I respond to what God reveals?

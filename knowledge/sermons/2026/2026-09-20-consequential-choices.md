# Consequential Choices

**Speaker:** Geoff Prows  
**Date:** 2026-09-20  
**Primary Scripture:** Matthew 7:13-23 (NASB 1995)  
**Series:** Summer on the Mount: Kingdom Living for the Here and Now

## Message Summary

As Jesus begins His conclusion to the Sermon on the Mount, He does not introduce another ethical command. Instead, He calls His listeners to respond to everything He has taught. Geoff Prows emphasized that no one can remain neutral toward Jesus. Every person must choose whether to follow the way of the world or the way of Christ.

Jesus presents three pictures with consequential outcomes: two roads, two trees, and two confessions. The broad road is popular and unrestricted, but it leads to destruction. The narrow road is costly and sometimes uncomfortable, but it leads to life. Jesus therefore calls people not merely to hear about the narrow gate, but to enter it and follow Him.

Jesus then warns about false prophets who appear to belong to the flock but are inwardly destructive. Because false teaching can sound mostly true, believers must look beyond appearances and evaluate the fruit produced by a teacher's life and ministry.

Finally, Jesus warns that correct words, correct theology, and even impressive religious activity are not themselves proof of salvation. Genuine faith is more than an intellectual profession. A transformed life is increasingly marked by obedience to Christ through the work of the Holy Spirit. Obedience is not the basis of salvation, but it is evidence of belonging to Jesus.

## Main Idea

Jesus calls every person to make a consequential choice: leave the broad way of self-rule and follow Him on the narrow road that leads to life, discerning truth by its fruit and possessing a genuine faith that is evidenced by obedience.

## Message Outline

### I. Two Roads With Different Destinations

Jesus contrasts the broad road that leads to destruction with the narrow road that leads to life.

The broad road is easy to find, popular, and spacious enough to accommodate whatever a person wants to believe or pursue. It promises pleasure, fulfillment, and happiness, but ultimately leads away from God.

The narrow road is different. Jesus describes a restrictive gate and a difficult way. Following Christ can bring discomfort, cost, and opposition, but this road leads to life. In Christ, believers receive new life, abundant life, and eternal life.

Geoff emphasized that it is not enough to know about the gate or hear preaching about the road. A person must actually enter through the gate and follow Jesus. Everyone is already traveling one of these roads, but someone on the road to destruction can turn around, repent, and follow Christ.

### II. Two Trees With Different Fruit

Jesus warns His followers to beware of false prophets. False teachers may look like part of the flock and may say many things that are true, making them difficult to recognize at first.

Their teaching subtly broadens the narrow way. Rather than confronting sin and calling people to faithfulness, their message may consistently soothe, flatter, or avoid the difficult truths of Scripture.

Jesus says they will be recognized by their fruit. The nature of a tree eventually becomes visible through what it produces. In the same way, the long-term fruit of a teacher's character, doctrine, and ministry reveals what is at the root.

This calls Christians to be careful about the teachers, preachers, podcasts, media voices, and ministries they follow. Something sounding good is not enough. Believers should pay attention to both what is taught and what is produced.

### III. Two Confessions With Different Eternities

Jesus then describes people who make an impressive profession of faith, calling Him "Lord, Lord" and pointing to remarkable religious works done in His name.

Geoff stressed the sobering point that a person can know correct facts about Jesus and still not belong to Him. James 2:19 shows that even demons possess correct intellectual beliefs about God. Genuine saving faith is therefore more than intellectual agreement.

Religious activity is not enough either. Preaching, ministry activity, or other outward works cannot substitute for a genuine relationship with Christ.

Jesus points to a life marked by obedience to the Father's will. This is not works-based salvation. Obedience does not earn salvation; rather, it is evidence of a transformed life and of the Holy Spirit's work within a believer.

By contrast, a life consistently characterized by self-rule and disobedience gives reason for serious spiritual examination. Jesus' words, "I never knew you," describe someone who never truly belonged to Him, not someone who once possessed salvation and later lost it.

## A Call to Respond

The three pictures all press toward a decision.

Jesus calls people to turn from sin and self-rule and receive Him as Lord and Savior. Those who recognize that they are on the wrong road do not have to remain there. They can repent, turn to Christ in faith, and receive the life He offers.

For believers who have drifted in their walk with Christ, the message also calls them back to faithful obedience and close fellowship with Jesus.

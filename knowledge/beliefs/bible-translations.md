# What Bible translation does Urbancrest use?

## Short Answer

Urbancrest does not require a single English Bible translation. **Pastor Geoff most commonly preaches from the New American Standard Bible (NASB), while Pastor Dave typically uses the New King James Version (NKJV).**

Both translations lean toward **formal equivalence**, often described as a "word-for-word" approach.

## What Translation Does Pastor Geoff Use?

Pastor Geoff most commonly preaches from the **New American Standard Bible (NASB)**.

The NASB leans strongly toward formal equivalence, seeking to preserve the wording and structure of the original biblical languages as closely as clear English allows.

## What Translation Does Pastor Dave Use?

Pastor Dave typically preaches and teaches from the **New King James Version (NKJV)**.

The NKJV also leans toward formal equivalence, seeking to closely represent the wording and structure of the original languages while using updated English.

## Does Urbancrest Require One Bible Translation?

**No.** Urbancrest does not require everyone to use the NASB, NKJV, or any single English translation.

Our conviction is that **the Bible is God's inspired and authoritative Word**. The Baptist Faith & Message affirms the inspiration, truthfulness, and authority of Scripture but does not prescribe one English translation.

## What Is a Word-for-Word Translation?

"Word-for-word" is a common shorthand for **formal equivalence**. This approach places greater emphasis on preserving the words, grammar, and structure of the Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek texts when translating them into English.

No responsible translation is mechanically one English word for every original-language word. Languages work differently, so translators must still make careful judgments about grammar, context, and meaning.

## What Is a Thought-for-Thought Translation?

"Thought-for-thought" commonly refers to **functional or dynamic equivalence**. This approach places greater emphasis on expressing the meaning of a phrase or sentence in natural contemporary English.

Formal-equivalence and functional-equivalence translations exist on a spectrum rather than as two completely separate categories. Every translation must consider both wording and meaning.

## Why Do Our Pastors Often Use Word-for-Word Translations?

Translations that lean toward formal equivalence can be especially useful for **preaching and close Bible study** because they allow readers to see more of the wording and structure reflected in the original biblical languages.

That is one reason the NASB and NKJV are regularly used in Urbancrest's preaching and teaching.

## Are Other Bible Translations Okay to Use?

**Yes.** You do not need to own an NASB or NKJV to participate at Urbancrest.

Faithful English Bible translations can be valuable for reading and study, and comparing translations can sometimes help clarify how a passage is being expressed. Our pastors may also reference other translations when teaching.

## Detailed Answer

Urbancrest's confidence is in **Scripture itself**, not in one required English Bible translation.

Pastor Geoff most commonly preaches from the NASB, and Pastor Dave typically uses the NKJV. Both lean toward formal equivalence, which makes them particularly useful for verse-by-verse preaching and close study.

Other faithful translations can also be useful. Formal-equivalence translations emphasize the wording and structure of the original languages, while functional or thought-for-thought translations emphasize communicating the meaning naturally in English. Both approaches involve careful translation decisions.
